Hedging and Margin

The minimum-variance hedge ratio — which is Cov/Var on supplied data — and the margin arithmetic behind a futures position.

Scope note. Problem A is a covariance-and-variance calculation wearing a derivatives label, so it sits squarely inside the “basic statistical functions” the instructor asked students to review. Problem B is arithmetic on a price path. Neither requires a formula you have to recall unaided.

In Excel

  1. Compute COVARIANCE.S, VAR.S, STDEV.S and CORREL for the two series.
  2. Form h = COVARIANCE.S / VAR.S. Check it against SLOPE(ΔS,ΔF) and against CORREL × STDEV.S(ΔS) / STDEV.S(ΔF) — three routes, one number.

Enter your results


h = 0.323324 / 0.36897 = 0.8763. N = 0.8763 × 180,000 / 15,000 = 10.5155, so 11 contracts.

Notice what the hedge ratio does. A naive one-for-one hedge trades 12 contracts; the minimum-variance calculation says 11. The correction comes from the volatility ratio — σ(ΔS) is 0.5454 against σ(ΔF) of 0.6074, so futures move more than spot and fewer contracts are needed. Correlation is high at 0.9759, so it is the volatility difference driving the adjustment rather than weak co-movement.

And h is a regression slope. It is the same Cov/Var estimator that produces beta in the market model and the market-model slope in an event study.

Interpretation

1. Two traders, same profit, different experience

Trader A holds a futures contract and Trader B an otherwise identical forward. Both end with the same total profit. Explain why A may nevertheless be worse off.

The profits are equal only at maturity. The futures position is marked to market daily, so its cash flows arrive as a stream of margin debits and credits rather than a single settlement. If the price path moves against Trader A early, margin calls demand cash at exactly the moment the position looks worst. That is a funding and liquidity cost the forward does not impose: capital is tied up, other opportunities are foregone, and in the extreme a solvent position is closed out because the trader cannot meet a call. Identical terminal payoffs, different risk. This is the mechanism behind the LTCM failure Jorion (2000) documents — the positions were arguably right, and the financing was not.

2. What survives the hedge

After placing the minimum-variance hedge, what exposure does the fabricator still carry, and why can more contracts not remove it?

Basis risk — the residual variation between the spot price of the alloy actually bought and the futures price of the standard grade. Adding contracts changes the size of the futures exposure, not the correlation between the two prices, and it is the imperfect correlation that generates the residual. The hedge ratio minimises the variance of the hedged position; it does not drive it to zero unless ρ = 1. There is also quantity risk if the eventual purchase differs from 180,000 units, and rounding to a whole number of contracts leaves a small unavoidable mismatch.
